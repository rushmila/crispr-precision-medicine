# CRISPR in Precision Medicine: Transforming Gene Editing with Artificial Intelligence

**Author:** Rushmila Islam (rushmila.islam@gmail.com)

This repository hosts a review paper on how artificial intelligence is advancing CRISPR-based genome editing for precision medicine. It covers AI-driven guide RNA design, off-target prediction, delivery optimization, and the ethical and clinical challenges of bringing personalized gene therapies to patients.

## Abstract

Healthcare is moving away from a one-size-fits-all model toward precision medicine. Precision medicine aims to tailor medical treatment and interventions to the individual characteristics of each patient, taking into account variability in genes, environment, and lifestyle. At the core of this revolution is Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats (CRISPR) based genome editing, a technology that has given researchers a direct, programmable way to correct the genetic defects underlying hereditary diseases. While CRISPR provides the programmable molecular scissors required to execute precise genomic modifications, deploying this technology safely and efficiently at a clinical scale presents monumental challenges, notably in predicting off-target effects, designing optimal guide RNAs, and understanding complex cellular repair mechanisms. This is where artificial intelligence (AI) comes in. By leveraging machine learning algorithms and deep neural networks, AI can rapidly process vast genomic datasets to identify optimal target sites, predict the precise outcomes of DNA repair, and engineer safer, higher-fidelity CRISPR variants. Together, CRISPR and AI could overcome the current limitations of gene editing and make personalized therapies a reality.

This paper examines the role of AI in genome editing with CRISPR towards an advancement in precision medicine. It provides an overview of CRISPR genome-editing technologies and their applications in personalized healthcare, discusses the major technical, ethical, and clinical challenges limiting current systems, and explores how AI is being used to enhance guide RNA (gRNA) design, improve editing specificity, optimize delivery systems, and develop new genome-editing tools. Finally, the review highlights future perspectives and emerging opportunities at the intersection of CRISPR and AI that are expected to shape the next generation of personalized medicine.
